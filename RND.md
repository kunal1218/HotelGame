# Game R&D — Research, Design, and Experimental Concepts

**Project:** Untitled Multiplayer Game  
**Document:** Research & Development  
**Version:** 0.1 — Initial brainstorming  
**Status:** Pre-production / Experimental  
**Source:** November 2026 brainstorming session

---

# 1. Executive Summary

The project is an experimental multiplayer game focused on unpredictable exploration, meaningful player decisions, changing social relationships, and entertaining emergent situations.

The initial vision involved five players exploring a mysterious apartment building while avoiding hunters.

Over the meeting, the idea developed into several overlapping concepts:

- A supernatural hotel containing unpredictable rooms
- A continuous labyrinth filled with strange encounters
- A competitive race through progressively more dangerous regions
- A morality and karma system involving cooperation and betrayal
- A mysterious hotel acting as the last safe shelter after a catastrophe
- An AI game director that introduces challenges based on current gameplay

The team has not finalized which concept will become the finished game.

The strongest development agreement was to **prototype small mechanics, test them with friends, and expand only when the experience is demonstrably fun**.

## Design philosophy

The game should create situations where players make difficult decisions with incomplete information.

Its entertainment should come from the interaction between game systems, unpredictable events, and human behavior rather than exclusively scripted content.

The team also wants gameplay that naturally produces memorable clips, which can support organic social-media marketing.

# 2. Product Identity

## 2.1 Intended experience

The game should make players feel:

- Curious about what is behind the next door
- Uncertain about other players' intentions
- Motivated to pursue personal objectives
- Pressured by enemies, deadlines, and resources
- Tempted to accept risky opportunities
- Excited when unexpected situations develop
- Amused by chaotic interactions with friends
- Afraid during selected supernatural encounters
- Satisfied when risky decisions succeed

## 2.2 Game inspirations

|---|---|
| Lethal Company | Multiplayer exploration, unpredictable encounters, funny reactions |
| Fall Guys | Racing through stages, setbacks, accessibility |
| Hello Neighbor | Navigation and avoiding a roaming hunter |
| Scooby-Doo | Chaotic chases through interconnected doors |
| Backrooms | Surreal architecture and uncertainty |

## 2.3 Target experience

Initial assumptions:

- Multiplayer with approximately five players
- Real-time movement and interaction
- Relatively short time to first interesting event
- Readable controls and objectives
- Strong replay potential
- Multiple viable strategies
- Memorable social interactions
- Horror elements without requiring constant terror

Exact session duration, target platform, audience, camera perspective, and multiplayer model remain undecided.

# 3. Core Gameplay Pillars

## 3.1 Exploration

Players should want to open doors and discover new places.

Rooms may contain:

- Valuable resources
- Optional challenges
- Strange NPCs
- Money-making opportunities
- Hidden paths
- Equipment
- Unusual environmental events
- Enemy encounters
- Story clues
- Seemingly ordinary spaces

An ordinary room is acceptable when it contributes to pacing or uncertainty.

However, repeated empty corridors should not become the dominant experience.

**Design goal:** Exploration itself must be entertaining.

## 3.2 Meaningful Decisions

Players should regularly face competing incentives.

Examples:

- Finish an objective or rescue a friend
- Gamble money or preserve existing resources
- Trust another player or work alone
- Steal a resource or negotiate access
- Risk a dangerous shortcut or take a longer route
- Reveal information or keep it secret
- Help maintain the base or pursue personal gain

Choices should have consequences without guaranteeing one universally correct answer.

## 3.3 Social Competition

The game may combine cooperation and competition.

Players can:

- Form alliances
- Share equipment
- Negotiate trades
- Make informal agreements
- Compete over scarce resources
- Sabotage opponents
- Betray alliances
- Rescue one another
- Refuse to help

Whether matches have one winner, multiple winners, or cooperative victory remains open.

## 3.4 Escalating Pressure

Players need reasons to act instead of hiding indefinitely.

Pressure systems under consideration include:

- Timed objectives
- Hunter patrols
- Resource shortages
- Progression gates
- Survival needs
- Threat escalation
- Limited equipment
- Rival player progress
- Hotel maintenance requirements

## 3.5 Emergent Humor and Horror

The game should alternate between social comedy, urgency, and scary encounters.

Possible situations:

- A player runs toward a friend, unaware the voice belongs to a monster
- Someone gambles away the team's resources
- A betrayal occurs immediately after a deal
- A monster interrupts a complicated task
- Two allies accidentally trap each other
- A seemingly harmless room contains a bizarre event

# 4. World and Setting

## 4.1 The Hotel

The hotel is the most consistent setting across the discussion.

Possible functions:

- Shared starting area
- Tutorial
- Social hub
- Character introduction space
- Equipment storage
- Upgrade location
- Safe shelter
- Connection to surreal rooms or dimensions

The hotel should initially appear relatively ordinary or luxurious.

Its true nature may gradually become apparent.

## 4.2 Supernatural Labyrinth

One proposed world model is a connected sequence of unusual rooms.

Properties:

- Doors can connect to surprising environments
- Areas need not conform to normal physical geometry
- Some locations may be rearranged procedurally
- Players can map and revisit discovered spaces
- Certain areas may be more dangerous or valuable
- A home base can potentially advance deeper into the structure

## 4.3 Seven Sins / Descent

Another proposed model features a progression through seven regions inspired by the Seven Deadly Sins.

The original theme includes:

- Pride
- Greed
- Lust
- Envy
- Gluttony
- Wrath
- Sloth

The group later preferred environments that encourage morally questionable actions rather than rigidly segregating every room by sin.

A region may have a recognizable central theme but still include unrelated room types.

Possible environments:

- Luxury hotel
- Casino
- Nightlife district
- Lawless city
- War zone
- Abandoned hospital
- Unusual residential space
- Supernatural underworld

The final confrontation with Satan was discussed as a possible endgame.

**Status:** Long-term conceptual direction, not final scope.

## 4.4 Wasteland and Alternate Dimensions

A later concept involved the hotel surviving a catastrophic event.

Players emerge into a devastated environment while the hotel remains functional.

The world outside could contain:

- Ruined urban areas
- Deserts
- Dangerous NPC groups
- Water sources
- Salvage sites
- Mutated creatures
- Supernatural portals
- Unexpected dimensions

The hotel acts as a shelter maintained through resource gathering.

**Status:** Alternative core-loop candidate.

# 5. Character Classes

The group moved toward four archetypes.

Classes should create distinct gameplay opportunities without forcing players into predetermined moral behavior.

## 5.1 Businessman

**Theme:** Money and negotiation

Potential strengths:

- Better trading rates
- Improved financial rewards
- Resource efficiency
- Negotiation advantages

Potential weaknesses:

- Limited direct combat ability
- Less specialized crafting knowledge

Primary playstyle: Economy and bargaining.

## 5.2 Teacher

**Theme:** Knowledge, creativity, and assistance

Potential strengths:

- Additional crafting knowledge
- Discovering recipes
- Repairing objects
- Identifying useful resources
- Helping other players

Potential weaknesses:

- Limited fighting ability
- Lower financial starting advantage

Primary playstyle: Crafting, problem-solving, and support.

## 5.3 Police Officer / Security Guard

**Theme:** Authority and combat

Potential strengths:

- Weapon handling
- Defensive abilities
- Combat effectiveness
- Protecting other players
- Interacting with security-related systems

Potential weaknesses:

- Limited economic advantages
- Potential reputational consequences for abuse of authority

Primary playstyle: Combat and control.

## 5.4 Socially Persuasive Character

**Theme:** Influence and manipulation

Potential strengths:

- Persuading NPCs
- Negotiating favors
- Influencing social encounters
- Recruiting temporary allies
- Unlocking dialogue options

Potential weaknesses:

- Lower direct combat effectiveness
- Reliance on interactions

Primary playstyle: Social strategy.

## 5.5 Class design constraints

Initial recommendations:

- One unique ability per class for the prototype
- Every player retains basic movement and interaction
- No class should be required for every objective
- Class advantages should be useful but limited
- Each class should have opportunities to succeed
- Roles should support unexpected behavior

Cosmetic customization may be added later.

# 6. Karma and Moral Choices

## 6.1 Overview

Karma represents the consequences of player decisions.

Possible positive actions:

- Rescuing others
- Sharing resources
- Honoring agreements
- Helping NPCs
- Repairing shared systems

Possible negative actions:

- Stealing
- Sabotaging
- Betraying others
- Exploiting NPCs
- Unprovoked attacks

## 6.2 Reward structure

The discussion suggested that immoral actions might provide immediate benefits but lead to long-term consequences.

For example:

- Stealing yields quick currency
- Helping produces future favors
- Betrayal provides an immediate advantage
- Cooperation improves relationships
- Low karma attracts additional supernatural attention

A pacifist approach might reduce danger while sacrificing certain rewards.

## 6.3 Region-dependent morality

One proposed mechanic is that the game's rules change as players approach hell.

Early regions may heavily penalize serious violence.

Later regions may make dangerous or selfish behavior more rewarding or less restricted.

This changes the strategic environment as players progress.

## 6.4 Open design questions

- Is karma public or private?
- Does karma persist across matches?
- Can players recover lost karma?
- Does the AI director use exact karma values?
- Can players exploit the system by repeatedly helping and betraying?
- Does karma determine enemy targeting?
- Should the same action have different consequences in different regions?

**Status:** Important concept, requires prototype testing.

# 8. Objectives and Progression

The biggest recurring unresolved problem was defining the actual core objectives.

## 8.1 Candidate A — Competitive Race

Players advance through regions and attempt to finish before others.

Progression gates block access to the next region.

Each gate requires certain conditions to be satisfied.

Players may cooperate, compete, or sabotage others to satisfy those conditions.

Death causes a setback rather than permanent elimination.

Potential win condition: First to escape or reach the final destination.

## 8.2 Candidate B — Hotel Survival

The hotel serves as a central shelter.

Players must gather resources from dangerous exterior environments.

Possible shared requirements:

- Electricity
- Water
- Food
- Medical supplies
- Security
- Shelter maintenance

Failure to maintain the hotel creates danger.

Potential win condition: Survive for a target period, complete an evacuation objective, or progress to an additional region.

## 8.3 Candidate C — Labyrinth Escape

Players explore a large interconnected maze.

They must locate routes, retrieve required objects, and complete tasks.

Hunters create pressure.

Potential win condition: Discover and unlock a final escape route.

## 8.4 Candidate D — Hybrid

Players maintain the hotel while advancing through strange regions.

Shared objectives preserve survival, while private objectives produce competition.

This combines the strongest elements of the other models, but introduces greater complexity.

**Status:** No core loop officially selected.

# 9. Hunters, Enemies, and Horror

## 9.1 Basic hunter behavior

Possible actions:

- Patrol
- Investigate sounds
- Pursue players
- Search recently occupied rooms
- React to suspicious behavior
- Guard resources
- Respond to AI director events

## 9.2 Capture

Proposed elements:

- Short capture animation
- Horror-style visual presentation
- Audio cues
- Respawn delay
- Progress setback
- Possible resource loss

Permanent elimination was discussed but became less favored in the competitive race concept.

## 9.3 Deceptive enemies

Possible abilities:

- Fake footsteps
- Voice mimicry
- Misleading noises
- Disguises
- Manipulation of environmental signals

Voice cloning or replay would require player consent and safeguards around audio capture, storage, and misuse.

## 9.4 Horror pacing

The game is not necessarily pure horror.

The team wants horror to support tension, comedy, and social gameplay.

Recommended principles:

- Do not rely only on jumpscares
- Give threats recognizable behavior
- Let players learn patterns
- Use surprise selectively
- Avoid unavoidable punishment
- Preserve moments of safety

# 10. AI Game Director

## 10.1 Concept

An AI system evaluates the current match and chooses events intended to create interesting situations.

Potential inputs:

- Player locations
- Player health
- Karma
- Inventory
- Current objectives
- Proximity of hunters
- Time since last encounter
- Team alliances
- Resource availability
- Progression state

Potential outputs:

- New encounter
- Hunter investigation
- Environmental event
- NPC offer
- Optional challenge
- Secret objective
- Spawn opportunity
- Room connection change

## 10.2 Architecture recommendation

Separate the AI director from the game simulation.

**Game engine responsibilities:**

- Movement
- Collisions
- Combat
- Pathfinding
- Inventory
- Networking
- Objective validation
- Damage
- Basic NPC behavior

**AI director responsibilities:**

- Suggesting events
- Selecting encounter types
- Choosing narrative opportunities
- Influencing pacing
- Producing optional challenges

All director actions should be validated by deterministic game rules.

## 10.3 Fairness constraints

The AI should not have unrestricted authority.

Restrictions may include:

- No spawning directly on top of a player without warning
- No blocking every possible escape route
- No impossible objectives
- No arbitrary removal of earned progress
- No excessive targeting of one player
- No constant interruptions
- No manipulation of already confirmed player actions

The goal is interesting gameplay, not maximum punishment.

## 10.4 Practical implementation

An initial director could be entirely rule-based.

Possible event selection:

1. Read current match state
2. Identify valid event types
3. Filter out unfair events
4. Score remaining opportunities
5. Randomly choose among suitable events
6. Trigger the event
7. Apply an encounter cooldown

An LLM-based planner could be evaluated later.

This reduces development complexity and avoids depending on model latency for real-time combat.

# 11. Procedural Room Generation

## 11.1 Desired properties

- Varied room appearances
- Different activities
- Interconnected navigation
- Optional shortcuts
- Interesting hidden areas
- Unpredictable discoveries
- Fair paths to objectives
- Room types appropriate for progression

## 11.2 Generation strategies

Possible techniques:

- Room templates
- Weighted random selection
- Graph-based layouts
- Difficulty-based room pools
- Predefined special rooms
- Dynamic events inside existing rooms

## 11.3 Suggested room graph

Represent the world as a graph:

- Nodes represent rooms
- Edges represent doors or routes
- Special nodes represent objectives, safe rooms, gates, and encounters

The generation system can then enforce constraints such as:

- Every required objective is reachable
- Players have multiple possible routes
- The starting area connects to useful content
- Progression gates appear at intended points
- Dead ends provide reasonable rewards or clues

## 11.4 Dynamic room movement

The group proposed AI-directed changes to room locations or connections.

This remains experimental.

For fairness, previously explored spaces could remain stable while unexplored connections change.

Possible private information:

# 14. Tutorial and Onboarding

## 14.1 Opening premise

Five strangers arrive at a seemingly normal hotel.

They check in around the same time.

The atmosphere initially feels ordinary.

## 14.2 Proposed tutorial

The group suggested that players might:

1. Enter the lobby
2. Meet a receptionist
3. Receive a room assignment
4. Travel to their room
5. Complete an introductory interaction
6. Encounter an unusual event
7. Rejoin the other players

The exact event remains undecided.

## 14.3 Simpler implementation

Because individual character stories may be expensive to develop, a common introductory objective may be preferable.

Classes can still provide different ways to complete the same task.

Example:

**Objective: Obtain access to a restricted hotel corridor.**

- Businessman negotiates a fee
- Teacher repairs a broken access mechanism
- Police Officer uses a security-related interaction
- Social character persuades a guest or employee

This is an example for testing class differentiation, not a recorded group decision.

# 16. Replayability

The team recognized that truly endless entertaining gameplay is extremely difficult to design.

Instead of attempting unlimited dynamic generation from the beginning, replayability should emerge from a finite but varied collection of systems.

Potential sources:

- Different objectives
- Different room layouts
- Random encounters
- Character classes
- Different player choices
- Social alliances
- Resource scarcity
- Karma consequences
- Alternative routes
- AI-directed events

Replayability should be measured through repeated playtests rather than assumed.

# 17. Marketing and Shareability

A repeated goal was to create gameplay that players naturally want to clip.

Desired moments:

- Unexpected betrayals
- Risky gambles
- Frightening encounters
- Chaotic escapes
- Funny NPC dialogue
- Unplanned teamwork
- Dramatic recoveries
- Rare discoveries

Marketing considerations should shape encounter readability and pacing without replacing the need for fun core mechanics.

Important principle:

**A game that produces clips but is not enjoyable to play is not sufficient.**

# 18. Development Strategy

## 18.1 Prototype-first approach

The group repeatedly warned against overcommitting to a huge vision.

Development should focus on:

1. One small idea
2. One playable implementation
3. A group playtest
4. Feedback
5. A decision to improve, change, or abandon the mechanic

## 18.2 Existing prototype

The meeting described an early environment that appeared visually impressive and supported basic control.

The codebase, engine, network implementation, and feature completeness have not been independently inspected.

## 18.3 Collaboration

The group discussed:

- Sharing the repository
- Separate Git branches
- Shared backlog
- AI coding assistants
- Rapid experiments
- Integrating successful features

Recommended rule:

Large architectural changes should be discussed before multiple developers independently build incompatible systems.

# 21. Current Direction and Recommendation

The team has not selected a definitive core gameplay loop.

However, the following ideas appear especially important across the conversation:

- Multiplayer interaction
- Surprising exploration
- Distinct character roles
- Meaningful objectives
- Risk-reward decisions
- Temporary cooperation
- Opportunities for betrayal
- Dynamic threats
- A memorable hotel setting

The immediate goal should be to identify the smallest implementation that meaningfully tests these properties.

Avoid simultaneously implementing seven regions, an advanced AI director, a full crafting economy, procedural dimensions, character storylines, and sophisticated combat.

The strongest next milestone is:

**Create a playable multiplayer scenario where players have an objective, an incentive to leave safety, something to gain, something to lose, and a reason to interact with one another.**

# 23. Final Principle

The team should not assume that the largest or most complicated idea will produce the best game.

The goal is to discover a repeatable experience that is genuinely entertaining.

Build something small, play it together, observe what happens, and expand the parts that consistently create interesting decisions, surprises, tension, and social interactions.

**The game should earn its complexity through playtesting.**
